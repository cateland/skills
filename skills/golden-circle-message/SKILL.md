# Golden Circle message

Follow Simon Sinek's **Golden Circle** order: Why, then How, then What. The structure is the skeleton; the prose is **seamless**.

Draft only. The user sends every message themselves; never send or publish it.

## 1. Gather

Read any supplied source thread or document first. Resolve only what the request and sources leave open. If the `grilling` skill is available, use it. Otherwise, ask numbered questions in conversation, recommend answers where supported, and wait for answers before asking dependent questions. Look up accessible facts yourself; leave decisions to the user.

Cover:

- **Audience**: who reads it, and what they care about.
- **Channel and language**: Slack, email, or other; the intended language.
- **The change**: what is happening, and who it affects.
- **The belief**: the purpose behind it, in one sentence. This is the Why.
- **Status**: which parts are *decided* and which are *open for input*.
- **Desired reaction**: awareness, discussion, adoption, or a reply.
- **Scope**: what is out of scope, and what gets only a passing mention.

**Complete when:** every item has a source-backed answer or a user-confirmed answer or default. Confirm shared understanding after any questioning before drafting. Treat unresolved facts or decision status as questions, not defaults.

## 2. Build the skeleton

Plan three sentences, one per ring, keeping these working notes out of the delivered message:

- **Why**: the belief or the problem it answers, grounded in the audience's experience.
- **How**: the approach or principle that follows from the Why.
- **What**: the concrete actions, tools, people, and dates supported by the sources or user.

**Complete when:** the How addresses the Why and every What follows from the How. Repair any broken link before writing prose.

## 3. Write seamless prose

Apply every applicable technique:

- **Open on the tension.** Convey the Why through a short, concrete account of the problem or stakes in the team's own experience.
- **Transitions carry the logic.** Link the purpose to the approach, then the approach to action. Use Situation, Complication, Resolution from Minto's *Pyramid Principle* as the narrative thread while preserving the ring order.
- **Keep the scaffolding hidden.** Let the order carry the structure without framework names or ring labels. Emphasize only a few key terms for skimming.
- **Tone follows status.** State decided parts firmly. Frame open parts as proposals and ask for input on those parts.
- **People changes get one short paragraph.** When people move teams or roles, name who moves where and acknowledge the cost to the teams losing someone.
- **The What is concrete.** Give each action or tool a short, reader-facing benefit. Use a short list when the channel supports it.
- **Close on the desired reaction.** For discussion or a reply, ask one or two open questions and offer concrete help. For adoption, state the next step and offer support. For awareness, offer a route for questions without implying that settled decisions are open.
- **Fit the channel.** Slack uses `*bold*`, `•` bullets, and sparse emoji. Email gets a subject line and plain paragraphs. For other channels, follow the user's requested format or the channel's conventions.

Aim for about 300 words unless the user specifies otherwise. If the `stop-slop` skill is available, use it to revise the draft while preserving facts, decision status, and ring order.

**Complete when:** the draft follows all applicable techniques and fits the requested channel, language, and length.

## 4. Check and deliver

Re-read the draft against the skeleton and gathered context:

- Each ring is present, in order, and the How and What follow from the preceding ring.
- The framework stays unnamed and the working notes remain outside the message.
- Every decided or open item carries its matching tone.
- Names, actions, tools, and dates match the sources or the user's answers.
- Out-of-scope items are absent; passing mentions stay brief.
- The closing matches the desired reaction.

**Complete when:** every check passes. Deliver one copy-ready draft directly in the conversation, with the subject line included for email. Put any explanation of judgement calls in one or two lines outside the draft.

For revisions, edit and re-deliver the full message so the user always has one copy-ready version.
